## What it means

David Reed emphasizes group formation as a source of network value. [Reed, The Law of the Pack](https://hbr.org/2001/02/the-law-of-the-pack).

The combinatorial intuition can be derived directly. Each of N people is either inside or outside a subset, creating 2^N subsets. Remove the empty subset and N singleton subsets, and the number of possible groups with at least two members is **2^N − N − 1**.

That count is exact for this definition of a group. The claim that useful network value follows similar growth is a separate heuristic, with much stronger assumptions.

## Worked example

For five participants, there are 32 subsets. Excluding the empty group and five single-person groups leaves **26**. For ten participants, 1,024 minus one minus ten leaves **1,013**.

Imagine a workplace community. People can organize into project rooms, incident groups, study circles, and temporary working groups. The product supports useful coordination that a simple list of one-to-one contacts does not describe.

However, the same ten people cannot actively participate in all 1,013 groups. Many combinations have no purpose. Some groups duplicate one another, and additional notifications can consume attention. The arithmetic describes potential membership combinations, not the number of communities that will actually form or survive.

## Put it to work

Design around the cost of forming a useful group. Make purpose, membership, permissions, discovery, and an exit path understandable. A group that cannot find the right participants or retain context has little value merely because a database row exists.

Measure meaningful group activity: completed shared work, sustained participation, useful answers, and whether people return. Distinguish created groups from active ones. Segment by use case so one large inactive population does not conceal a few successful communities.

In an agent workspace, a shared project can combine people, documents, and tools around an outcome. Treat access control as a property of that real collaboration boundary. Automatically adding every available tool or participant can increase confusion and exposure without improving the group’s work.

## Limits and misuses

Reed’s law does not prove that revenue or customer value grows exponentially with signups. Attention, trust, relevance, moderation, and infrastructure impose constraints. Group memberships are also highly correlated rather than independent sources of equal value.

Compare this with Metcalfe’s pair-count intuition: pairs and groups illuminate different interaction opportunities, but neither count replaces evidence of benefit. Build mechanisms that help valuable groups form and function, then evaluate outcomes. A huge theoretical space is an invitation to choose well, not an instruction to instantiate every possibility.
