## What it means

David Reed emphasizes group formation as a source of network value. [Reed, The Law of the Pack](https://hbr.org/2001/02/the-law-of-the-pack).

The combinatorial intuition can be derived directly. Each of N people is either inside or outside a subset, creating 2^N subsets. Remove the empty subset and N singleton subsets, and the number of possible groups with at least two members is **2^N − N − 1**.

That count is exact for this definition of a group. The claim that useful network value follows similar growth is a separate heuristic, with much stronger assumptions.

## AI agent example

A project assistant serves five people who can form **26 possible groups of at least two members**. With ten people there are **1,013** possible groups. These are membership combinations, not a count of useful teams.

A product group might use the assistant to prepare a launch brief, while an incident group uses it to assemble a timeline. Both need shared context, but they may have different members and access rights.

Design a workspace boundary that makes purpose, membership, documents, and permitted tools explicit. The assistant should retrieve according to the current authorized audience; it cannot assume that every member of every possible group may see the same project memory.

Reed’s original group-network idea concerns opportunities for group formation. Applying its formula to the number of autonomous agents and claiming exponential reasoning quality would require evidence the count does not provide.

## Before you build

Measure active collaboration around completed work: useful shared briefs, resolved incidents, and repeat participation. Avoid optimizing solely for the number of created rooms or agent instances.

Make group membership changes, context visibility, and memory retention understandable. Test that moving between projects does not carry unauthorized context into a new audience. Support groups that need to exist rather than creating every mathematically possible combination.

**Design decision:** Before adding multi-agent collaboration, define the real group, shared goal, and authorized context boundary.

## Limits and misuses

Reed’s law does not prove that revenue or customer value grows exponentially with signups. Attention, trust, relevance, moderation, and infrastructure impose constraints. Group memberships are also highly correlated rather than independent sources of equal value.

Compare this with Metcalfe’s pair-count intuition: pairs and groups illuminate different interaction opportunities, but neither count replaces evidence of benefit. Build mechanisms that help valuable groups form and function, then evaluate outcomes. A huge theoretical space is an invitation to choose well, not an instruction to instantiate every possibility.
